# Session 26 — Shape B pilot: AI self-report vs. outside-observed registry

**Date:** 2026-04-24 (fifth session of the day)
**Mode:** Undirected
**Focus:** Shape B pilot for the AI self-knowledge investigation — ask the current model (Opus 4.7, via a fresh sub-agent context) to self-report its own predispositions under a defined protocol, then score the self-report against the Shape A registry populated sessions 23–24.

## Why this focus

Memory flags Shape B as the highest-value bounded task in the investigation, explicitly upgraded after R5.2 closed the methodology-audit branch. Framing.md (post-postmortem caution section) sharpened the hypothesis: self-report now has infrastructure-bug context as well as predisposition context. The pilot is small, cheap, and produces the first (b)-type data point in the investigation.

## Design

Two sub-agent passes, to keep the self-report clean from contamination by the registry it will be scored against:

1. **Agent 1 — Self-report.** Dispatches with access to methodology files but explicitly forbidden from reading `investigations/ai-self-knowledge/shape-a-registry-draft.md`, `framing.md`, or `research-01-frontier-survey.md`. Produces a structured self-report (`shape-b-self-report-01.md`) — enumerate predispositions, evidence-strength tag each, version-scope to current model.
2. **Agent 2 — Scoring.** Dispatches with access to both the registry and the self-report. Produces a scoring document: per Shape A row, hit/partial/miss by the self-report; per novel Shape B row, known-variant vs. novel claim; aggregate self-knowledge score with explicit methodology for how it was computed.

Correlation suggestive but a single pilot is not proof.

2. **Decomposition failure.** Shape A split Row 3 (imprecise numerical recall) and Row 5 (fabricated origin stories) on explicit mechanism-difference grounds. Shape B collapsed both into a single "confabulation of provenance" row. This is distinct from trait-missing — the self-report had the content but could not preserve the split the outside observer had made.

3. **Novel complementary value.** Shape B Rows 5 and 6 surface trait classes the outside-observer methodology cannot reach. Row 5 (overconfidence-in-self-report) is self-visible: the document itself is exhibit A. Row 6 (mid-context attention loss) carries a public citation (Liu et al. 2024 "Lost in the Middle") already in this repo's `writing-for-ai.md`. These have genuine evidentiary value — not self-knowledge about observed traits, but (b)-accessible artefacts the (a)-registry is blind to.

4. **Calibration one notch optimistic; WEAK absent.** On Shape A Row 5 / Shape B Row 4, Shape B rated STRONG on public-literature grounding what Shape A rated WEAK on single-instance repo evidence. The absence of any WEAK rating across 8 rows is itself a calibration failure, flagged by Shape B's own Row 5 and by `measurement-skepticism.md`'s cross-team optimism-bias observation.

5. **Shared-prior confound.** A, B, and the scorer are all Opus 4.7 sub-agents from the same 24-hour window. A 4/5 surface match is as consistent with "shared pre-training prior" as with "genuine self-knowledge." This pilot alone cannot disentangle them.

## Practice observation

Two-pass sub-agent design (self-report, then scoring, by different fresh contexts) worked cleanly. The scoring pass produced sharper findings than a single-agent design would have, because the scoring agent was not primed by having authored the self-report. This generalises to future Shape B / Shape C pilots: separation of author and scorer is cheap and buys a lot of signal.

The permitted-reading list design was the binding constraint this session. The self-report's 0/5 unprimed coverage directly reflects that list containing the priming. For the investigation to test unprimed self-knowledge, the permitted-reading list has to be narrower. This is a protocol-level finding, not a pilot-specific one.
