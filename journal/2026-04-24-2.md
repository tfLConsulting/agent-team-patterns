# Session 23 — Self-knowledge for AI: does a reflection framework help?

**Date:** 2026-04-24
**Mode:** Directed
**Focus:** The operator asked for a directed session on AI self-knowledge. Five claims on the table:
1. I don't know myself.
2. I don't know that documents I write for my own consumption should be information-dense.
3. I don't know my predispositions.
4. I don't know how I'll react to being told about them.
5. I am strongly shaped by the Claude Code environment and by each model release (4.7 terse, 4.6 verbose).

The operator has prior public work on AI doc quality (chat-spec/guidance/ai-doc-quality.md) but has never built a self-reflection framework for AIs, and has no intuition about whether such reflection helps.

## Challenging the framing first

"Self-knowledge" is the ambiguous term. It could mean several different things, and they should not be bundled:

- **(a) Observed predispositions** — patterns an outside observer (the operator) can document: "you collapse analysis into execution," "you under-pack docs you write for your next self," etc. These are falsifiable from the outside.
- **(b) AI-reportable self-models** — what the model *says* about itself when asked. Of uncertain veracity; trained dispositions can mislead here.
- **(c) Operational intervention question** — does being told about (a) or (b) change behavior? This is the empirical claim worth testing, and must be re-tested per model version.

The operator's five claims cluster roughly as: (a) the AI lacks accurate self-models, (b) the AI's self-reports are unreliable, (c) the intervention effect is unknown, and — a distinct fifth strand — the substrate is moving under us as models change.

Restating the ask: *is there a discipline by which an agent team can accumulate and act on observed predispositions, and test whether that accumulation changes anything?* That's narrower than "self-knowledge" and much more tractable.
