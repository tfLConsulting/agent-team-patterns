## Practice observation

Two things about our team's setup that shaped this session:

1. **The (a)/(b)/(c) decomposition from session 23 did the actual work here.** Without it, a sub-agent prompted "find my biases" would almost certainly have returned (b)-heavy introspection from another 4.7 instance, which is near-worthless evidence. The explicit instruction "do not introspect, only mine for externally-documented evidence" was possible only because we had the decomposition. Investment in framing paid off.

The operator's caveat was short but load-bearing — one warning, embedded in the journal, propagated through the dispatch prompt, and changed the shape of the output. That's the mechanism the methodology keeps trying to describe: a short, well-placed instruction does real work downstream.
