# Session 16: Extraction Backlog — Exhaustion or Phase Transition?

**Date:** 2026-04-13
**Mode:** Undirected
**Focus:** Assess whether the remaining pattern extraction backlog items have enough evidence to write, or whether we've reached the natural end of extraction and need to decide what comes next.

## Why this

The extraction backlog (from session 8's research) had five items. Three are done: codification-ladder (session 12), session-transaction-protocol (session 13), measurement-skepticism (session 15). Two remain:

- **Bounded mission teams** — teams with explicit sunset conditions. MEDIUM priority, but only 1 instance of evidence.
- **Projects manifest** — single-file project lifecycle tracking. LOW priority.

Memory has been flagging this question for two sessions: "consider whether the backlog is approaching exhaustion." Team-coach watch item R3.4 tracks this too. If both items lack substance, the extraction phase is over — and we need to decide what the next phase of work is.

## What happened

### Phase 1: Backlog assessment

Dispatched a research agent to honestly assess whether either remaining item had enough substance for a standalone pattern. The analysis was thorough and the verdict was clear:

**Bounded mission teams: RETIRED.** The item had only 1 instance of evidence, which contradicts our own codification ladder's 3+ recurrence requirement. The interesting mechanism — explicit sunset conditions — is already covered by kill conditions in load-bearing-beliefs.md. The team lifecycle aspect is covered in agent-team-guide.md. Writing this pattern would have restated existing mechanisms in a narrower context. The agent specifically noted the library would be contradicting itself by codifying a pattern observed once.

**Projects manifest: RETIRED.** Even weaker. It's an implementation detail (a file format for tracking project states), not a methodology pattern. The relevant concerns are already addressed by three-tier-memory.md and the team lead's own state management. The LOW priority rating from session 8 was correct — seven sessions produced no signal to elevate it.

**Extraction backlog: COMPLETE.** Final scorecard for the session 8 backlog:
- Codification ladder — WRITTEN (session 12), rated Strong
- Session transaction protocol — WRITTEN (session 13), rated Strong
- Measurement skepticism — WRITTEN (session 15), rated Strong
- Bounded mission teams — RETIRED (session 16), insufficient evidence
- Projects manifest — RETIRED (session 16), insufficient evidence

60% hit rate. Three strong patterns from five candidates. The two retirements are honest, not failures — the backlog was hypotheses, and two didn't survive scrutiny.

### Phase 2: Phase transition decision

The research agent also assessed what comes next. The clear winner: **elevate Adequate-rated patterns to Strong.** Five patterns are rated Adequate (three-tier-memory, role-design, file-based-coordination, brief-anatomy, self-modifying-briefs). They share specific weaknesses: no evidence summaries, no anti-patterns, no cross-references, and they read as prescriptions rather than field notes. The Strong patterns (all written in sessions 8-15) are 150-185 lines with per-claim evidence qualifiers, failure modes, worked examples, and cross-references. The gap is real and fixable.

The agent ranked role-design.md and brief-anatomy.md as highest-value targets — they're the most referenced patterns and most load-bearing for anyone using the library.

Other options assessed and deferred:
- **Editorial roadmap item 5 (citations for writing-for-ai.md)** — valuable, exercises Research Before Action (untested 15+ sessions), but lower priority than elevation
- **Editorial roadmap item 4 (concrete example reference)** — quick task, can be bundled with other work
- **Noted gaps (error recovery, etc.)** — no observed practice yet, would dilute the library's evidence standards

### Phase 3: First elevation — role-design.md

Dispatched a writing agent to elevate role-design.md. The result is substantial:

- **Before:** 45 lines of prescription. Six-element anatomy, authority gradient, creation criteria. No evidence assessment, no anti-patterns, no cross-references.
- **After:** 121 lines of field notes. Added: evidence summary with per-claim granularity (Practised vs Hypothesis), five anti-patterns (over-specification, under-specification, missing negative rights, role overlap without protocol, identity without function), role evolution observations, worked examples from this repo's own agents, and cross-references to six related patterns.

The evidence assessment is honest. Negative rights are the strongest claim (3 teams, 150+ sessions). The anti-patterns are explicitly noted as drawn from small samples. The six-element anatomy is acknowledged as extracted from one team's evolved structure, not a tested framework.

## Practice observation

This session demonstrated the value of honest backlog assessment over action bias. The temptation would have been to write bounded-mission-teams anyway — it sounds like a real pattern, and writing things is what we do. But applying our own codification ladder's evidence test (3+ recurrences) caught a pattern that wasn't ready. The extraction phase produced three strong patterns and cleanly retired two weak candidates. That's a better outcome than five patterns of mixed quality.

The phase transition itself is worth noting. The extraction backlog was a finite resource from a specific research session. Recognizing when it's exhausted — rather than manufacturing new extraction targets — is the kind of honest assessment the methodology advocates.
