# From a team recipe to a design library

The library's focus changed from standing agent teams to the reasoning behind
planning, execution, and disposition. The new material separates principles from
applications and provides a route for another repository to design locally.

Earlier files remain as historical evidence. Their assumptions about standing
roles, delegation, and fixed coaching cadence no longer govern this library.
AGENTS.md is current guidance, with CLAUDE.md pointing to it. Existing role commands
now route to the manual work record rather than restarting the earlier team.

The implementation includes a read-only ledger example with fictional input and
meaningful validation tests. It helps with selection and preparation; it does not
claim to execute agents, verify business outcomes, or replace authority judgments.

The detailed plan, evidence, disposition, and remaining uncertainty are in
[the work record](../work/2026-10-library-redesign.md). This journal is historical
narrative, not another source of current task status. No automation was installed.

Practice observation: the earlier patterns contain useful mechanisms, but their
operating instructions could silently reactivate unnecessary machinery. Explicit
current guidance and visible historical status make reuse more deliberate.

Next session should focus on evidence from a real adoption, or new user direction.

## Second pass: challenge the synthesis

A further review found that the initial library explained records better than
plan validity, information-strategy effectiveness, control calibration, and reopening
settled decisions. It also made manual-first adoption and the ledger's dependency
policy too easy to inherit as defaults. The second-pass work record separates the
portable considerations from local mechanisms and records the corrections.

The example packet now preserves findings after artifact acceptance. Contrasting
fictional cases expose choices the original fixture did not demonstrate. This is
self-review and a design correction, not evidence of effectiveness in a new project.
