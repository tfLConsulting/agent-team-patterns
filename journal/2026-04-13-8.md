# Session 17: Elevating Brief Anatomy

**Date:** 2026-04-13
**Mode:** Undirected
**Focus:** Elevate brief-anatomy.md from Adequate to Strong — add evidence summaries, anti-patterns, worked examples, cross-references.

## Why this

brief-anatomy.md is second on the elevation roadmap (after role-design.md, completed session 16). It's heavily referenced — the "briefable scope" test in role-design.md points to it, the coaching framework references it, and anyone bootstrapping a team uses it as a template. At 57 lines, it reads as prescription: "here's what a brief should contain." The Strong patterns in the library are 120-185 lines of field notes with per-claim evidence, failure modes, and cross-references.

The gap between what brief-anatomy.md claims and what it demonstrates is specific:
- No evidence assessment (which claims are tested vs. hypothesised?)
- No anti-patterns (what does a bad brief look like?)
- No cross-references (how does this connect to the rest of the library?)
- No worked examples (our own agents are living examples)

## What happened

Dispatched a writing agent to elevate brief-anatomy.md with full context: the current file, role-design.md as the quality target, both agent briefs as worked examples, and related patterns for cross-references.

The result is substantial:

- **Before:** 57 lines of prescription. Seven sections listed with "you should" language. No evidence assessment, no anti-patterns, no cross-references, no worked examples.
- **After:** 127 lines of field notes. Every section now carries inline evidence qualifiers (Practised with team count, or Hypothesis). Five anti-patterns added: The Prescriptive Brief, The Vague Brief, The Anchored Brief, Missing Negative Rights, The Frozen Brief. Worked examples from both team-lead and team-coach briefs. Seven cross-references to related patterns. Full evidence summary section at the bottom.

Key editorial decisions in the elevation:
- The "known bias" addition to team-lead's brief was surfaced as a practice observation — a useful addition that emerged organically, not part of the seven-section template
- Tool usage instructions exclusion honestly flagged as Hypothesis rather than Practised — it's reasoned from writing-for-ai principles but hasn't been directly tested
- The briefable scope test's limitation acknowledged — effective for clarity but not completeness
- Quantitative context claim noted as potentially confounded (teams that add numbers may also write sharper briefs overall)

The honesty about evidence strength is consistent with the library's standards. No inflation.

## Practice observation

The elevation roadmap is proving its value as a forcing function. Without it, the bias would be toward writing new patterns (more exciting) rather than strengthening existing ones (more useful). Role-design and brief-anatomy were the two most-referenced Adequate patterns — elevating them improves every other pattern that cross-references them. The compound effect of elevation exceeds the marginal value of a new pattern at this point in the library's maturity.
