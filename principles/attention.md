# Treat attention as a limited resource

**Principle:** spend human judgment where it changes an outcome. Additional agent execution can be cheaper than repeatedly reconstructing context for a person.

An attention item should explain the decision, evidence, credible alternatives, recommendation, consequences, and what happens if no decision is made. Separate a request for judgment from a request for access or a mechanical action. Route each to the person who can resolve it. Do useful authorised preparation before escalating.

Choose the grouping around the decision: related workstream results, a single urgent finding, a service exception, or a portfolio choice. A workstream summary can explain what changed, what was learned, what remains, and which decision follows. Keep underlying artifacts available. Ten green task reports can conceal a workstream that has solved the wrong problem.

One application batches ready decisions in a focused sitting, record each answer as it arrives, then execute the authorised decisions together. If execution reveals a materially different choice, return that item with its evidence rather than inventing consent. Small teams may prefer immediate decisions; batching helps only when it reduces waiting and context switching.

Standing policy can dispose of bounded routine work. Define applicability, exclusions, evidence requirements, owner, and how to suspend it. Sample accepted results and review exceptions. Repeated false alarms should lead to investigation of the instrument; broad suppression can hide real cases.

Track decision age, repeat asks, time spent reconstructing context, avoidable waiting, and whether important findings reached their reader. Do not optimise merely for fewer questions or a smaller queue: silence can mean effective delegation or lost information.

**Tradeoff:** more preparation consumes execution capacity and may delay an urgent escalation. Set urgency and preparation budgets. A missed deadline or new irreversible exposure may warrant an immediate, incomplete packet.
