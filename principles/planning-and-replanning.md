# Keep the plan worth executing

**Design consideration:** selection can reliably execute an obsolete plan. Decide how work enters the system, what it displaces, and what evidence causes the plan to change. A queue validator cannot answer whether a task is worth doing.

## Demand, capacity, and refinement

Capture the original need before translating it into a task. Separate an observation, a candidate, a commitment, and an authorised action. Review duplicates, affected owners, expected benefit, opportunity cost, and the smallest next action that resolves uncertainty. A task can be mechanically valid while prescribing the wrong solution or a deliverable its own scope forbids.

Ask whether a new task changes the workstream's direction or merely implements it. Preserve the original instruction while recording corrections to future definitions. Reviewers should be able to challenge the acceptance criteria and the value of the task, not only compliance with its wording.

Plan refinement can be triggered by elapsed time, new demand, completed work, stalled progress, changed assumptions, or an operator request. Choose triggers for the project's rate of change. A fixed review every few tasks is one policy; it is not the principle. A quiet queue can still contain a stale plan. Repeated refinement can also consume all delivery capacity.

Inspect useful outcomes, age and starvation while eligible, blocked work, priority concentration, abandoned directions, and time spent maintaining the system itself. Before adding work, explain what capacity it uses and what will wait. A growing queue is neither automatically healthy nor automatically a failure. Cancel, combine, re-sequence, narrow, or stop work when evidence warrants it.

## Evidence that does not arrive

Distinguish evidence not yet collected, evidence too expensive for the current budget, and evidence that the available methods cannot establish. Record what was tried, why it did not answer the question, which decision depends on the gap, and the cost of being wrong. Do not turn a budget limit into a claim that something is unknowable.

Choose whether to investigate, run a reversible experiment, decide under explicit uncertainty, defer to an observable event, or stop. Some questions deserve a terminal disposition: no further investigation is justified now. That is different from endlessly creating follow-up tasks so that every question appears answerable.

## Re-entry and supersession

Planning, execution, and disposition can recur within one task. An implementation defect may need a local repair; an insufficient design may reopen design work; a falsified premise may invalidate the direction and affect several workstreams. Record which decisions and dependent tasks need reconsideration. Completion of yesterday's prerequisite does not establish that its premise still holds today.

Keep the earlier decision discoverable as the decision made on the evidence then available. Link its replacement and propagate material changes to affected briefs, tests, guidance, and readers. A history-preserving edit or a new superseding document can both work. Urgent evidence invalidating a plan may need direct attention rather than waiting for a scheduled synthesis.

**Choices to keep local:** who can refine priorities; whether refinement preempts execution; mandatory decision gates; grouping work into streams; and when a direction change pauses dependent work.
